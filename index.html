Codices of the Realm: Jurisprudence in Antiquity

Synopsis & Scope

This documentary investigates the transformation of procedural, penal, and civil administrative apparatuses across Ancient and Medieval India. Analyzing legal codification from early Vedic assemblies (*Sabha* and *Samiti*) to the administrative edicts of the Maurya and Mughal administrations, the film deconstructs how political sovereignty drew validity from custom, religious edicts, and institutionalized jurisprudence.

Academic Deconstruction

Key Jurisprudential Themes

Institutions

Adjudication & Courts

The hierarchy of judicial mechanisms: King's court (*Dharmasthiya*), regional courts, guild tribunals (*Puga*, *Shreni*), and community elders (*Kula*), showing decentralized dispute resolution.

Substantive Law

Vyavahara (Civil Procedure)

Evaluation of the eighteen titles of law (*Ashtadasa Vivadapadani*), tracing early principles of contract, boundaries, debt, inheritance, and partnerships.

Evidence

Pramana (Evidentiary Standards)

Threefold categories of proof: Human testimony (*Sakshin*), written instruments (*Lekhya*), and possession/custom (*Bhukti*), supplemented by *Divya* (ordeals) only when evidentiary chains broke.

Bibliography & Archival Records

Primary & Secondary Sources

Arthaśāstra of Kauṭilya

Treatise on Statecraft

Book III & IV providing early codification of criminal suppression (*Kantakasodhana*) and civil adjudications (*Dharmasthiya*).

Yājñavalkya Smṛti

Dharmashastra Tradition

Noted for legal precision and structural development of legal procedure (*Vyavaharadhyaya*), heavily cited in medieval commentaries like the *Mitākṣarā*.

Fatāwā-e-Ālamgīrī

Mughal Legal Canon

The Hanafi-law-based legal compilation commissioned during the 17th century, governing imperial judicial administration.

Structural Context

Historical Timeline

c. 1500 BCE – 600 BCE

Vedic Assemblies & Customary Norms

Norms dictated through societal consensus; dispute resolution administered via collective bodies (*Sabha* and *Samiti*).

c. 320 BCE – 185 BCE

The Mauryan Bureaucratic Legal Framework

Centralized codification under Kautilyan doctrines; separation of civic-legal jurisdictions and deployment of royal edicts (*Sasana*).

c. 300 CE – 1100 CE

Smriti Commentaries & Procedural Refinements

Elaboration of court procedures, documentary execution, evidentiary rules, and succession rules by legal commentators like Vijnaneshwara.

c. 1206 CE – 1707 CE

Sultanate & Mughal Islamic Jurisprudence

Introduction of sharia courts (*Qazi*), secular decrees (*Jawabit*), and municipal police administrators (*Kotwal*).
